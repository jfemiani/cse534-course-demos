Purpose

Lesson 7.3 changed exactly one line of code (nn.RNN to nn.LSTM) and the resulting model beat the plain RNN's reference loss on every single one of the module's five held-out sentences. This discussion asks you to reason about the gating mechanism itself, and about what that one-line change did and did not actually fix.

Instructions

Pick one of the questions below. Write a post of 250-400 words. State your position clearly, and explain the reasoning behind it, referring to specific numbers or examples from 7.3's demo where you can. Then reply to at least one classmate who picked a different question, or who reached a different conclusion on the same question.

1. Which gate deserves the most credit?

An LSTM cell has three gates: forget, input, and output. 7.3 explains what each one does, but the demo never isolates them individually; it only compares the full LSTM against a full plain RNN. If you had to guess which single gate contributes the most to the improvement in Table 1 (especially on the longer sentences), which one would you pick, and what about how gradients or information flow through a cell state makes you think so?

2. Gating fixed the numbers, but did it fix the translation?

The LSTM's reference loss improved on every sentence, but its greedy translations are still not correct French (for example, "je sais mon père." for "I know my job."). Reference loss and translation quality are clearly not the same thing here. What is reference loss actually measuring, and what would you need to change about this demo (data, training, or evaluation) to see gains that show up as better translations, not just a lower loss number?

3. Gating didn't remove the bottleneck. Does that matter?

7.3's takeaway is that gating made better use of the same fixed-size encoder-decoder bottleneck, without removing it: the long sentence was still the hardest one for the LSTM, same as it was for the RNN. Argue for or against this claim: is a well-managed bottleneck (LSTM) fundamentally different from a removed bottleneck (Transformer, coming in 7.4), or is it just a matter of degree? What kind of evidence, beyond what 7.3 shows you, would settle that question?

4. Why would variety in the greedy guesses matter?

7.3 points out that the LSTM's five greedy translations were all noticeably different from each other, while the RNN's greedy translations collapsed to nearly the same phrase for four of the five inputs. Why would that variety, on its own, be meaningful evidence about what the model learned, separately from whether reference loss improved? Can you think of a way a model could have low reference loss but still collapse to repetitive greedy output, or high reference loss but produce varied greedy output?
