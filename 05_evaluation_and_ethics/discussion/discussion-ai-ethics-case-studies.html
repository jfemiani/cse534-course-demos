Purpose

Lesson 5.3 raised several questions about AI ethics without answering any of them. This discussion is where you answer one, in your own words, and then respond to someone who answered differently.

Instructions

Pick one of the three questions below. Write a post of 250-400 words. State your position clearly, and explain the reasoning behind it. Then reply to at least one classmate who picked a different question, or who reached a different conclusion on the same question. Disagree respectfully; the point of this discussion is to see how other people reason about the same situation, not to reach a single class consensus.

1. Personal messages written by AI

Suppose someone uses AI to write a condolence note, a wedding speech, or an apology, and the result is just as good as anything they could have written themselves. Is anything lost? If so, what exactly is lost, and does it matter whether the recipient ever finds out AI was involved?

2. The New Hampshire robocall

In January 2024, a robocall using AI-generated audio of President Biden's voice told New Hampshire voters not to vote in the state's primary. The audio was not labeled as AI-generated, and the incident led to real legal consequences. Would a label have mattered here? Where should the line be drawn between political speech that is allowed and political speech that is not, when AI can now generate a convincing fake of anyone?

3. Video that is harder to doubt

Most people already know photos and text can be faked, but video still feels harder to doubt. As AI-generated video gets more convincing and easier to make, what responsibility do platforms have to label it? What responsibility do viewers have to check before they believe it or share it?
