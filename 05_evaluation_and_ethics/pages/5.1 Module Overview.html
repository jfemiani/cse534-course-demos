How do you know it's any good?

Every earlier module built something: a probability model, a prompt, a tool call, a retrieval pipeline. This module asks the question that comes after building anything. How do you know if it works? How do you know if it works better than the version you had before?

This module has two halves. The first half is technical. You will measure a model's raw ability, and you will measure your own design choices, using real numbers instead of guesses. The second half is not technical at all. Evaluating AI raises questions about people, work, and trust that a benchmark score cannot answer. Both halves count as "evaluation" in a broad sense, and this module treats them as two separate lessons.

Key Topics

  • Cross-entropy and perplexity, measured on text a model never trained on
  • Reading a results table the way a research paper presents one, including what it does not tell you
  • Benchmarks for large models: what they test, and where to find current scores
  • Head-to-head human judgment, as a different kind of measurement from a benchmark
  • Why benchmarks go stale, and what benchmark contamination means
  • Ablation studies and grid search, as ways to measure your own design choices
  • The costs of AI systems, and open questions about work, trust, and consent

Activities

This module includes a quiz covering both lessons, a discussion built around some of the ethics case studies in lesson 5.3, and a lab on evaluation methods. The ethics questions on the quiz do not ask you to pick a "right" answer about what is ethical. They ask you to identify which concern a scenario raises. The discussion is the place for your own position; state it, and respond to a classmate's.

Reading

Lesson 5.2 does not follow a textbook. Benchmarks and evaluation tools change too fast for a fixed chapter to stay current, so that lesson links to live leaderboards and current documentation instead. Lesson 5.3 links to Generative Deep Learning's closing chapter for one perspective on responsible AI, alongside the sources named in that lesson.
